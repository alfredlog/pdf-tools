    What is OCR?

    OCR stands for “optical character recognition”. To a computer, a scan or a phone photo is just an image – you can’t search it or copy text from it. OCR recognizes the letters in the image and places the recognized text invisibly over the page. The original stays unchanged, yet search, selection and copying work.

    Why do you need a searchable PDF?

    For a paperless office and digital filing, to quickly find a passage in old contracts, bank statements and letters, to reuse text from a scan in Word – and because many authorities, accountants and application portals prefer searchable PDFs. Screen readers can also only read PDFs that have a text layer, so OCR makes documents more accessible.

    Tips for good text recognition

    Scan at 300 dpi or more, straight and without shadows. For phone photos, good daylight helps. Handwriting is only recognized to a limited extent; printed text in German, English and French works very reliably. Pages that already contain text are left unchanged.

    Frequently asked questions

    Does OCR change how my PDF looks?

    No. The pages stay identical. Only an invisible text layer is added.

    Can I copy the text into Word afterwards?

    Yes. Select the text in your PDF viewer and copy it, or convert the searchable PDF with “PDF to Word”.

    Which languages are recognized?

    German, English and French – also mixed in one document.

    Is handwriting recognized?

    Only to a limited extent. Text recognition is made for printed text.

    What happens to my document?

    It is transferred encrypted, processed and deleted right afterwards.
